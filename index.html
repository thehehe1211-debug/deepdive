ZONE 01 / SUNLIGHT
0 — 200 METRES

WHERE THE LIGHT LIVES

Sunlight fills the upper ocean, warming the water and helping tiny drifting plants grow. This bright layer is home to coral reefs, turtles, dolphins and countless fish.

ZONE 02 / TWILIGHT
200 — 1,000 METRES

THE BLUE BEGINS TO FADE

Only a little sunlight reaches this far. The water gets colder, and many animals rise toward the surface at night to feed before returning to the dim depths.

Sunlight is too weak for most plants to grow in this layer.
1,000 M BELOW THE SURFACE
ZONE 03 / MIDNIGHT
1,000 — 4,000 METRES

DARKNESS HAS A GLOW

Sunlight can no longer reach this zone. Some animals make their own light through bioluminescence — a tiny signal in an enormous darkness.

The Titanic rests around 3,800 metres below the surface.
4,000 M BELOW THE SURFACE
ZONE 04 / ABYSSAL
4,000 — 6,000 METRES

A WORLD OF EXTREMES

The pressure is immense and the temperature is close to freezing. Food is scarce, so life here survives with remarkable adaptations and patience.

Some deep-sea animals can go a very long time between meals.
6,000 M BELOW THE SURFACE
ZONE 05 / HADAL
6,000 — 11,000 METRES

ALMOST ANOTHER PLANET

Deep ocean trenches cut into the seafloor. Very few people have explored these places, and every expedition can reveal something unexpected.

The Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench is about 10.9 kilometres down.
10,900 M BELOW THE SURFACE
YOU REACHED THE BOTTOM

THE DEEPEST DARK IS STILL FULL OF LIFE

You travelled from sunlit water to one of Earth's most extreme environments. The ocean is vast, mysterious, and still waiting to be understood.
